"The Signal" — Shot-Level Breakdown
Every scene decomposed into individual shots, second-accurate, written for two uses: (1) Flux still-frame generation as init images, (2) WAN 2.2 motion generation from those inits. Tone target: Interstellar — practical-feeling analog/digital hybrid tech, teal/amber grade, volumetric light, quiet gravity, camera that holds rather than cuts frantically.

0. Visual Style Guide (lock this before generating anything)
Cast
M — Mathematician: 50s, close-cropped silver hair, weathered calm face, deep navy flight suit with a worn embroidered insignia, thin wire-frame glasses that catch console light. Moves deliberately, speaks in short declarative lines. Reference feel: a Cooper-mentor figure — quiet authority, not flashy.
F — Friend: late 20s, short dark hair, olive field jacket over a grey thermal, sleeves often pushed up, expressive hands mid-gesture. Leans in when explaining. Reference feel: the sharp one who makes the complex feel obvious.
Y — You: mostly represented as camera POV / over-the-shoulder silhouette. When face is needed: plain flight jacket, breath faintly visible, hands holding a slate/tablet. Everyman/everywoman audience surrogate.
Sets
Ops Control Room: circular chamber, wraparound curved monitor bank, central holo-table, warm amber practicals against cool blue ambient wash, dust motes suspended in light shafts, deep shadow falloff at the edges of frame.
Cockpit/Ship interior: tighter, analog toggle switches under glass, small round viewports, warm interior light that shifts to red-alert wash during tense beats.
Screen inserts (math): pure black background, glowing cyan-white equations, no clutter — legibility over decoration.
Camera grammar (use consistently across all scenes)
Y-POV: handheld-but-stabilized, subtle breathing sway — this is the audience.
OTS Y→M / OTS Y→F: classic over-the-shoulder, soft foreground blur of Y's shoulder/collar.
Two-shot M+F: symmetric framing with Y implied just off-camera between them.
Screen insert: locked-off, static, no camera motion — the one place stillness is correct.
Push-in: reserved only for revelations/emotional beats, never for teaching beats.
Reaction cutaway: close on Y or F's face watching a screen, used to keep humans present even during math-heavy beats.
Grade: teal shadows, warm amber highlights, light film grain, gentle anamorphic horizontal flare off any screen source.
Golden rule for math scenes: never let a Manim insert run alone for its full duration. Open on a human gesture toward the screen, hold the insert, close on a reaction. The audience should always feel like they're watching M and F work the problem, not watching a slide.
