Course Flow: "The Signal" — Complex Numbers → De Moivre's → Quadratic Expressions → Theory of Equations
The overarching problem statement
A deep-space listening post picks up a repeating, garbled transmission. The crew (Mathematician + Friend + You) has to:
Decode the raw signal (it arrives as pairs of numbers) → Complex Numbers
Correct the ship's course to follow the signal's bearing, which requires repeated rotations → De Moivre's Theorem
Plan safe burn windows so the ship doesn't overshoot or collide while chasing it → Quadratic Expressions
Crack the signal's final cipher, which turns out to be a polynomial encoding the destination coordinates → Theory of Equations
Every module below is a checkpoint in that same mission — nothing is taught "because it's next in the book," it's taught because the crew needs it to move forward.

#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story

De Moivre's Theorem — integral & rational indices
Ship needs to compute the result of many repeated rotation burns without simulating each one individually
Direct payoff: raising a complex number to a power = the shortcut for repeated rotation

nth roots of unity, geometric interpretation
Crew discovers the signal source isn't one point — it's broadcasting from several evenly-spaced relay stations in a circle
Roots of unity are literally equally spaced points on a circle — visual and narrative click perfectly here

Chapter close: Ship is now on course toward the relay stations. But fuel is limited — the crew needs to plan exactly when and how hard to burn thrust, or they'll overshoot or run dry.

Chapter 3 — Quadratic Expressions ("The Burn Window")
#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story
3.1
Quadratic expressions & equations in one variable
Fuel-burn trajectory naturally comes out as a quadratic — Friend models "distance vs time" for the burn
Grounds the quadratic in a real curve the crew can see on a screen, not an abstract equation
3.2
Sign of quadratic expressions, max/min values
Crew needs to know: does the trajectory ever go negative (i.e. crash course)? What's the most fuel-efficient point?
Sign changes = danger zones, max/min = the optimal burn moment — stakes make the concept concrete
3.3
Quadratic inequations
Mission control defines a "safe corridor" — the trajectory must stay within a range, not hit one exact value
Naturally frames inequations as a range the ship must stay inside, not just an equation to solve

Chapter close: Ship arrives safely. Now the crew has the full signal and must crack its final cipher — which the Mathematician reveals is a polynomial equation.

Chapter 4 — Theory of Equations ("Cracking the Cipher")
#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story

Relation between roots and coefficients
The cipher is a polynomial — Friend shows you can extract clues about the destination without fully solving it, just from the coefficients

Solving equations when roots are connected by a relation

Directly mirrors how these problems are normally posed — now it's a decryption clue instead of a textbook instruction
4.3
Real coefficients — complex roots in conjugate pairs
Some decoded coordinates come out complex — Friend explains why they always arrive in pairs, which is the final clue that the signal source is symmetric (two relay stations, not one)
Ties directly back to Chapter 1's complex numbers — nice narrative full-circle
4.4
Transformation of equations, reciprocal equations
Final step: the cipher needs to be transformed into a related equation to reveal the true coordinates
Climax — this is the module that "solves the mission"

Story resolution: Crew decodes the final coordinates, arrives at the source, mission complete. Each of the 14 sub-topics was a tool the crew needed, in the order they needed it.
