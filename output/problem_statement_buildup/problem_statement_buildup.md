Course Flow: "The Fold-Space Signal" — Complex Numbers → De Moivre’s Theorem → Quadratic Expressions → Theory of Equations
The overarching problem statement
A derelict research station in a "folded" dimension is broadcasting a faint, multi-dimensional distress signal. The crew (Mathematician + Friend + You) must intercept the signal, navigate the ship through a fluctuating gravitational anomaly, and crack a high-level polynomial cipher to open the station's airlock.
*   Interpret the raw multi-dimensional data stream → Complex Numbers
*   Sync with the spinning signal relay stations → De Moivre’s Theorem
*   Navigate a safe flight corridor through heavy gravity → Quadratic Expressions
*   Decrypt the final sequence of the station's security gate → Theory of Equations

Every module below is a checkpoint in that same mission — nothing is taught "because it's next in the book," it's taught because the crew needs it to move forward.

Module Flow
Chapter 1 — Complex Numbers ("Signal Acquisition")
#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story
1.1
Complex number as an ordered pair of real numbers & Fundamental operations
The ship's sensors pick up a stream of raw data pairs; the crew must learn to add and merge these fragments to form a coherent message.
Establishes: A complex number is essentially just a pair of numbers; we need to know how to manipulate them to build the message.
1.2
Representation of complex number in the form a + ib
The navigation computer cannot process "pairs"; it requires a standardized input format to recognize the signal's components.
Converts the raw data into a standardized "a + ib" format so the software can "read" the signal.
1.3
Modulus and Amplitude of a complex number-Illustrations
The crew needs to determine how "loud" the signal is and its specific bearing angle to aim the receiver.
Modulus = Signal Strength; Amplitude = Bearing Angle. These are the physical properties needed to lock on.
1.4
Geometrical and Polar representation of complex number in Argand plane - Argand diagram
The crew needs to see the signal's location on a 2D map relative to the ship's current position.
Translates the numbers into a literal star-map (Argand diagram) so the crew can visualize the target.

Chapter close: The crew has a visual lock on the signal. However, the signal is spinning at a high frequency, causing the ship's tracking software to lose its grip.

Chapter 2 — De Moivre’s Theorem ("The Spin Sync")

Story beat

2.1
De Moivre’s Theorem - Integral and Rational Indices
The signal is rotating rapidly; the crew needs a way to calculate the ship's required rotation at any given moment without manually tracking every degree.
Provides the shortcut for repeated rotation (powers), allowing the crew to sync with the spinning frequency.

nth roots of unity-Geometrical Interpretations-Illustrations
The crew discovers the signal is being bounced off a ring of multiple relay stations, all perfectly spaced in a circle.
Identifies the relay stations as "roots of unity"—equally spaced points that define the signal's source perimeter.

Chapter close: The crew is synced with the relays. Now they must physically travel to the center, but a massive gravitational fold is warping the space in front of them.

Chapter 3 — Quadratic Expressions ("The Gravity Fold")
#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story
3.1
Quadratic Expressions, Equations in one Variable
The ship’s flight path through the gravity well is governed by a quadratic curve; the Friend models the ship's position over time.
Grounds the quadratic equation as a "pathway" on the screen that the ship must follow.
3.2
Sign of quadratic expressions - Change in signs and Maximum and Minimum values
The crew must identify "dead zones" where the gravity becomes lethal (negative signs) and find the point of peak velocity to clear the fold.
Sign changes = Danger zones; Max/Min = The optimal "jump" point to clear the gravitational barrier.
3.3
Quadratic Inequations
Mission control identifies a "safe corridor"—the ship must stay within a specific range of altitude and speed to avoid being crushed.
Frames the math as a "safe range" (inequality) rather than just a single point, making it a survival requirement.

Chapter close: The ship successfully clears the fold and arrives at the station. However, the airlock is sealed by a massive, multi-variable polynomial cipher.

Chapter 4 — Theory of Equations ("The Final Cipher")
#
Topic
Story beat
Why this module exists in the story
4.1
Relation between the roots and the coefficients in an equation
The cipher is too long to solve directly; the Mathematician shows how to extract clues about the "key" just by looking at the outer coefficients.
Provides a shortcut to analyze the "DNA" of the lock without needing to crack the whole thing yet.
4.2
Solving an equation when two or more of its roots are connected by certain relations
The cipher contains a "buddy system" hint: certain coordinates are linked (e.g., one is a multiple of another).
Directly applies the math to a logical clue found within the alien security system.
4.3
Equations with real coefficients - occurrence of complex roots in conjugate pairs and its consequences
The final keys appear in pairs; the Friend explains that because the coefficients are "real," the complex keys must be symmetrical.
Uses symmetry to confirm that the keys are correctly decoded, providing the final proof for the crew.
4.4
Transformation of equations - Reciprocal equations
The final security gate is "inverted"; the crew must transform the entire cipher into its reciprocal form to turn the key.
The final climax: a mathematical "flip" that unlocks the door and completes the mission.

Story resolution: The airlock hisses open, revealing the precursor data. Every module from complex coordinates to polynomial transformations was a necessary tool to navigate the fold and unlock the truth.
